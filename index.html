Where Do Operations
PhDs Land?*

Evidence from 2,087 verified first jobs, PhD cohorts 1953–2026

A faculty-based sample of 3,161 people. Coverage is incomplete; these are not population placement rates.

Assistant Professor of Management

kostas.stouras@ucd.ie

Abstract

This project documents PhD careers in Operations Research, Operations Management and Industrial Engineering, starting with Operations Academia and reviewing university pages, CVs and placement reports, with OM List cross-checks and individual LinkedIn reviews. The current faculty-based sample contains 3,161 profile records and 2,087 verified first jobs. 432 first jobs (20.7%) are postdocs, while 1,013 (48.5%) are classified as tenure-track. A further 94 documented first jobs are in industry, government or nonprofits. Job type remains undocumented for 385 verified first employers (18.4%). These shares use all verified first jobs as their denominator; they are not population placement rates. Faculty-based selection, unresolved first jobs and incomplete career histories limit generalisation, particularly to graduates who remain outside academia. 180 career review records are published, including 4 access blockers without verified roles. Official-source university reviews are complete for 531 of 676 imported records. Partial histories, missing dates and unresolved identities remain explicit.

Keywords: doctoral education; academic careers; operations; industrial engineering.

06

How the data is collected

The same six steps as a placement census, applied to a directory-based sample: collect public sources, structure them with stable identifiers, validate every change, publish versioned files, classify first jobs by explicit rules, and say plainly what is and is not available.

6.1

Collection and cross-checking

We start with the Operations Academia university directory to discover universities and operations departments, then collect public professional information over time through website crawling and individual review of university faculty pages, personal academic websites, dated CVs, placement reports, appointment announcements and employer biographies. We use the OM List faculty directory and PhD-by-year listings to cross-check profiles and align names, universities and doctoral information. Individually reviewed, identity-matched LinkedIn profiles supplement career histories as self-reported evidence. Each check supports only the facts it records: matching a directory entry does not verify an entire profile, degree or first job. Search results are leads; evidence pages must be opened and read. Alignment checks and full career reviews are ongoing, and discrepancies remain explicit until resolved. Faculty-based coverage is not a census of all graduates, particularly those entering industry.

6.2

Structuring

Each person has a stable identifier that survives corrections. Profile records, verified first jobs and research dispositions are kept separately, with evidence links, review dates and unresolved questions. Organisation records connect universities, departments and first employers while retaining source names and aliases; missing facts are never inferred. Each record retains when it was first published and last changed, separately from the dates its sources were checked.

6.3

Validation

Research contributors write separate proposal files; one integrator reviews conflicts and merges by stable identifier, so a newer review can correct an older one but nothing is overwritten silently. The merge refuses records without a substantive reason, opened sources, an evidence link or a valid job type, and flags undocumented changes to a degree year or institution. Every deployment re-runs the checks: dataset identity, ledger coverage of every person exactly once, agreement between each published first job and its review record, and the relational schema tests. Corrections keep the original recorded value next to the verified one, with the reason.

6.4

Publication

Public updates appear after reviewed work is integrated and validated; the citation identifies the version date. Inspect the sources linked to individual records. Send corrections, source suggestions or removal requests to kostas.stouras@ucd.ie.

6.5

Classification

The first job is the first new appointment after the doctorate. A postdoc before a faculty job is the first job; a job that began well before the degree and simply continued is not, so the next new job counts; a post-doctoral appointment that starts in the final months before formal conferral counts, with the overlap noted; an announcement written before the degree establishes nothing. Types are tenure track, postdoc, teaching track, other academic, industry, government, non-profit, or "type not reported" when a source names the employer but not the kind of appointment (a university name alone does not make a job tenure track). Teaching track covers explicit adjunct, teaching, practice and instruction appointments; this does not establish that research ceased. Visiting and other academic roles remain separate. Start years are recorded only when documented, never copied from the cohort year.

6.6

Data availability

Counts load with the data.

The LinkedIn history pilot records accessible employment entries for a small number of people, with check dates and source links. A complete graduate census, doctoral-advisor dataset, publication-at-graduation dataset and comprehensive career panel remain uncollected. Map locations and countries are name matches to Wikidata items, kept with their identifiers so a wrong match can be corrected.

Coverage limitations and interpretation

This is a directory-based view, not a complete census of PhD graduates or jobs. Faculty outside the source, industry careers, and unreported appointments may be absent. Cohort counts describe only directory members with a known PhD year. They should not be interpreted as graduating class sizes or employment rates. Every share shown on this site is a share of verified first jobs, and the unresolved and not-yet-reviewed people are reported beside it. Source classifications are not independently verified degree specializations.

Graduate census and industry careers

The graduate census will begin with a historical register of Operations Management, Operations Research and Industrial Engineering doctoral programs, including closed and renamed programs. We will enumerate graduates by program and year regardless of career destination, then add employment histories, doctoral advisors and publications through the PhD year. Each fact will carry its own sources and last-checked date. Industry entry and later moves out of academia will be recorded as separate career events. Publications through the PhD calendar year will be distinguished from publications known to precede the actual degree date. Current coverage includes faculty profiles, partial graduate lists and individually reviewed placements and career histories.

Privacy and source attribution

This site republishes public professional information: names, institutional affiliations, academic roles, and PhD information where available. It does not publish personal contact details. No advertising, analytics, or tracking cookies are added by this application. Hosting providers may maintain operational logs.
